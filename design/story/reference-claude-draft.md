# The Hollow — lore

## Inspiration, and where we stop

Inspired by Haruki Murakami's *Hard-Boiled Wonderland and the End of the World*.

**What we take (themes and structure):**
- Two worlds that turn out to be one mind.
- A quiet walled town at the end of the world, calm but strangely empty of feeling.
- A newcomer who has to give up their shadow at the gate.
- A reader in an old library slowly deciphering something no one else can.
- The final question: stay in a peaceful place without your whole self, or leave with everything you are.

**What we don't take:** names, characters, specific objects or plot. No unicorns, skulls, Gatekeeper, Librarian, Calcutecs or Dreamreaders. Everything below is our own. That keeps the game original and out of copyright trouble.

## Two worlds

**The Waking Side.** The child's ordinary, noisy life. Never shown directly. Fragments drift into the Archive: a school bell, someone calling a name, a phone buzzing. Later chapters could alternate between the two sides, like the novel's two narrators.

**The Hollow.** A small town ringed by a tall wall, at the very bottom of the child's mind. The weather follows the child's heart.

Places:
- **The Gate** — the only way in. A lamp burns over it day and night.
- **The Old Archive** — dusty, sunlit, full of empty shelves. The Book is kept here.
- **The Handless Clock** — a tower clock with no hands. Time in the Hollow doesn't move until the Book is read.
- **The Still River** — flows into the wall and never comes out the other side.
- **Moth-lanterns** — small moths that glow. They gather around the reader when a page is solved.

## Arrival

The child wakes outside the Gate in a traveler's cloak too big for them. The **Lamplighter**, an old keeper with silver shears, says no one may enter with a shadow, and snips it loose.

The shadow doesn't disappear. It breaks into four pieces that slip into the town and hide. These are the inner demons. **The demons are part of the child.** That's why you can't destroy them, only face them.

## People

- **The Reader** (player) — a little boy or girl dressed like a fantasy traveler: oversized hooded cloak, satchel of quills, a small lantern.
- **The Lamplighter** — keeper of the Gate. Not cruel. Believes a shadowless life is a painless one.
- **The Archivist** — looks after the Archive and brings the Reader tea. Has forgotten how to feel things. As the Book is transcribed, they start to remember.

## The Book of the Hollow

The town's holy book, written in the **Old Tongue**, which no one living can read. Each folio holds a memory the town has lost. Transcribing it gives the memories back, including the child's own.

### Rules of the Old Tongue (gameplay)

- One symbol = one word. No articles ("the", "a"); the English translation adds them.
- Early symbols look a bit like what they mean (child = small figure, light = sun). Later chapters get more abstract.
- Later grammar marks: a dot for plurals, a slash for past tense, a ring for "not".

## Ending

The last folio is solved. The four shadow-pieces gather at the Gate. The Lamplighter offers a choice: stay in the Hollow, calm and painless and without a shadow, or leave with the shadow, demons and all.

The Reader leaves with it.

> It's okay to have inner demons. Take it one step at a time.
